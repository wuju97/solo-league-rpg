World History

The World: Solonia

The entire world is called Solonia, though most people simply call it The Nine Kingdoms, since it has been ruled by nine major kingdoms for centuries, each controlling its own region. The kingdoms are politically separate, but they share the same oceans, skies, history, and ancient past.

The Ancients

Long before the kingdoms existed, there was only one civilization — the First Civilization, known today only as The Ancients. They possessed knowledge far beyond the modern kingdoms: floating cities, impossible magic, giant constructs, living crystals, ancient beasts. Nobody knows what happened to them. They weren't destroyed or conquered — they simply disappeared. Only ruins remain, and many believe their greatest secrets still lie buried beneath the world.

The Three Moons

Unlike Earth, Solonia has three moons:

  • Lumis — a large white moon that controls the tides
  • Nyra — a purple moon that appears brighter near Mordrath, for reasons scholars don't understand
  • Veyl — a small blue moon, almost invisible, only appearing on certain nights

Many legends say important events happen when all three moons align.

The Four Great Seasons

Instead of months, the world uses four great seasons:

  • Bloom (Spring)Travel eases, festivals occur
  • Blaze (Summer)Best season for wars, harvests, exploration
  • Emberfall (Autumn)Kingdoms prepare for winter, monster migrations begin
  • Frostwake (Winter)Northern kingdoms isolated, some bosses only appear now

The Ancient Roads

Scattered across Solonia are ancient stone roads. Nobody built them, nobody repairs them, yet they never crumble. Many lead to ruins that don't appear on modern maps.

World Reputation

Every player has a World Reputation, and it's kingdom-specific — there's no universal "good" or "evil." Helping Valoria often builds their trust; attacking Valorian soldiers makes them suspicious. Meanwhile, Mordrath might respect you more for the exact same act. No kingdom is completely good or completely evil — even Mordrath has innocent citizens, and even Valoria has corruption. Players decide whom they trust.

The Living World Connection

Now that all nine kingdoms exist, Solonia is no longer a collection of locations — it's a connected, living world. Trade caravans move between Valoria and Khar'Duun, pirate fleets raid Azure Reach, scholars from Aetheria seek forbidden knowledge in Mordrath, healers from Eldergrove travel to Frosthaven during harsh winters, and master smiths from Pyrath supply equipment across the continent. Wars, alliances, disasters, discoveries, festivals, and migrations can ripple from one kingdom to another, creating stories that span the entire world rather than remaining isolated within a single region.

With this phase complete, the Nine Kingdoms of Solonia are established as the foundation of the game world.

Kingdoms

These kingdoms slowly rose over centuries, each developing completely different cultures. Some are allies, some enemies, some barely speak to each other — and some secretly plot against the others.

❄ Frosthaven — Kingdom of Ice (Ice)

Known for honor, discipline, and survival. Produces ice crystals, furs, and elite soldiers.

🌳 Eldergrove — Kingdom of Nature (Nature)

Known for healing, druids, and ancient forests. Produces herbs, rare wood, and nature magic.

🏰 Valoria — Kingdom of Humanity (Light)

The largest economy and the center of politics and trade. Most travelers begin their journey here.

☁ Aetheria — Sky Kingdom (Storm)

Floating islands, airships, magic, and weather research.

🌋 Pyrath — Fire Kingdom (Fire)

Master blacksmiths and the best weapons in Solonia. Volcanic mines power the largest metal production of any kingdom.

🔮 Mordrath — Kingdom of Shadows (Shadow)

Nobody fully understands it. Its borders slowly expand every generation, and strange disappearances occur near its frontier. No kingdom openly invades Mordrath.

🏜 Khar'Duun — Ancient Desert Empire (Sand)

The oldest surviving kingdom, filled with buried cities. Most treasure hunters eventually travel here.

☠ Blackfen — The Swamp Kingdom (Poison)

No official ruler — even maps disagree on its shape. Entire villages disappear without explanation.

🌊 Azure Reach — Ocean Kingdom (Water)

The largest navy in Solonia. Trade routes, pirates, exploration, fishing, and sea monsters.

The Elemental Wheel

Attacking the next element in the cycle deals +25% damage; being attacked by the element before you means taking +25% damage. All other matchups are neutral.

Fire → Ice → Nature → Sand → Water → Storm → Poison → Shadow → Light → (back to Fire)

All kingdoms are connected through the Azure Sea. Ships travel between kingdoms and trade routes constantly shift — pirates attack merchants, and sea monsters occasionally destroy entire fleets.

Valoria

The Crown of Solonia — capital Crownfall, a constitutional monarchy of ~12 million, mostly human with elves, dwarves, halflings, and rare beastkin. Crownfall is the safest city in Solonia.

Valoria isn't the strongest, richest, or most magical kingdom — it's simply the most stable. Nearly every merchant, explorer, and adventurer eventually passes through, since most roads lead here.

Geography: Valoria sits at the center of Solonia, bordering Frosthaven, Eldergrove, Pyrath, and Azure Reach — making it the trading heart of the world.

Economy: Exports steel, grain, wine, tools, books, and armor. Imports magic crystals, rare herbs, exotic woods, volcanic ore, and ancient relics.

Religion: The primary faith is the Church of the First Flame, which teaches that the First Flame gave humanity knowledge after the Ancients disappeared. Its major cathedral, the Cathedral of Dawn, stands in Crownfall.

Laws: Major crimes include murder, treason, smuggling, illegal magic, and slavery. Punishments vary by severity, and trials are held publicly.

Festivals: Spring Festival, Harvest Festival, Founder's Day, Knight Tournament, Royal Parade, Winter Lights, Monster Hunter Championship.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Iron PalaceSeat of the King
  • Grand ArenaLargest PvP arena — players can compete here
  • Hall of HeroesStatues of legendary warriors
  • The Endless LibraryOver one million books, several forbidden

Frosthaven

The Kingdom That Never Kneels — capital Winterhold Keep, a military monarchy of ~5.8 million, mostly human with mountain dwarves, frost elves, and rare Iceborn.

Frosthaven doesn't admire wealth — it admires strength. Every citizen learns hunting, survival, first aid, and basic swordsmanship, even nobles. Its lesson above all: "Survive today. Protect tomorrow."

Geography: The northernmost kingdom, with eight months of snow every year — frozen lakes, massive glaciers, pine forests, ice caves, and mountain passes. Avalanches constantly reshape the roads.

Economy: Exports ice crystal, silver, furs, medicinal herbs, and whale oil. Imports books, grain, wine, and magic artifacts.

Religion: The Eternal Flame — people gather around sacred fires during winter, where fire symbolizes hope, not destruction.

Festivals: Longest Night, Ice Hunter Tournament, Aurora Festival, First Snow Celebration, Winter Market.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Frozen ThroneAncient ice palace, builder unknown
  • Hall of ChampionsStatues of legendary warriors — none built while the honoree lives
  • The Endless GlacierLargest glacier in Solonia — rumored Ancient city beneath
  • The Blue ChasmBottomless — many expeditions have vanished into it

Living World Features: Frosthaven changes dramatically with the seasons. During Frostwake, frozen lakes become roads, ice caves open, certain monsters emerge, and rare bosses awaken. During Blaze, glaciers melt, new mountain paths appear, trade increases, and hunting becomes easier.

Mordrath

The Eternal Twilight, also called The Kingdom Without Dawn or The Whispering Realm — capital Noctis, an absolute monarchy. Official population estimates say 4 million; unofficially, nobody knows — even Valoria's census has failed here.

The First Rule: nobody truly understands Mordrath, not even its own citizens. Maps slowly become outdated — roads appear, buildings disappear, entire villages move. Yet people continue living normal lives.

Geography: unlike every other kingdom, Mordrath isn't completely dark — it's permanently trapped in late evening. The sun never fully rises and the sky never turns black; it remains an endless twilight, with purple clouds drifting overhead and stars visible even during "day."

National Identity: other kingdoms fear Mordrath, but its citizens don't understand why. To them, life is ordinary — children laugh, markets open, people fall in love, farmers grow crops, dogs play. Only strange things happen, and everyone simply accepts them.

Economy: exports void crystal, shadow silk, moon orchids, black glass, dream ink, and nightwood. Imports food, steel, medicines, livestock, and wine.

Religion: no official religion. Instead, citizens believe "everything has a shadow" — and the shadow remembers, even if the person forgets.

Festivals: Festival of Silence (the entire kingdom stays quiet for one hour), Night Bloom (a once-a-year flowering celebrated in the streets), and The Long Evening (nobody sleeps, markets stay open, music fills the city).

Famous Landmarks:

  • Mirror LakeShows reflections of places that aren't there
  • The Whispering ForestTrees occasionally whisper names — sometimes yours
  • The Hollow BellNever rung, yet everyone remembers hearing it
  • The Black ArchiveContains books that write themselves
  • The Ninth WallOnly eight walls are visible — nobody knows why it's named this

Gameplay Identity: unlike other kingdoms, Mordrath's quests emphasize investigation, moral choices, ancient ruins, recovering forbidden artifacts, solving mysteries, strange disappearances, and unexplained phenomena.

Azure Reach

Also called The Kingdom of Endless Horizons, The Ocean Crown, or The Thousand Isles — capital Blueharbor, population ~8.2 million. Governed by a Maritime Council led by the Grand Admiral — unlike most kingdoms, there is no king; the sea belongs to everyone.

National Identity: "The sea rewards the brave." People don't ask "Where are you from?" — they ask "What have you discovered?" Children grow up sailing, even nobles know how to navigate, and maps are considered priceless.

Geography: unlike every other kingdom, Azure Reach isn't one landmass — it's hundreds of islands: large, small, hidden, and floating, plus coral cities, sea caves, and entire villages that exist only on the backs of giant turtles.

Economy: the largest trading nation. Exports fish, pearls, ships, salt, sea silk, coral glass, whale oil, and navigation charts. Imports steel, wood, magic crystals, grain, and medicine.

Religion: the Tide Mother, believed to guide sailors — every ship carries a small shell blessed by the priests.

Festivals: Festival of Tides, Ship Regatta, Ocean Lantern Night, Whale Migration, Explorer's Day, Treasure Festival.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Endless LighthouseVisible from 100+ km, builder unknown
  • Coral CathedralBuilt beneath the sea, accessible only at low tide
  • The Spiral MaelstromA permanent whirlpool — many ships disappear, few return
  • The Glass OceanCrystal-clear waters where ships appear to float in the sky
  • The Leviathan GraveyardHundreds of gigantic skeletons — cause of death unknown

Ships: players can eventually own ships — Fishing Boat, Scout Sloop, Merchant Brig, Explorer Frigate, War Galleon, the rare Sky-Sail Hybrid, and the legendary Living Coral Ship. Owning a ship unlocks new routes, hidden islands, and sea-based questlines.

The Great Mystery — The Silent Horizon: sailors whisper that if you sail far enough west, the sea becomes perfectly still — no wind, no waves, no birds, no stars. Every expedition that has entered has either returned with no memory of what happened, or never returned at all.

Eldergrove

Also called The Living Kingdom or The Emerald Realm — capital Sylvaris, population ~6.5 million. Governed by the Council of Roots: seven Elders chosen from different walks of life, where decisions require consensus, reflecting the belief that harmony is stronger than authority.

National Identity: "Nature is not beneath us. We are beneath nature." Citizens never cut a healthy tree without permission from the local Wardens. Animals are considered citizens of the kingdom, and even monsters have a place in the natural balance.

Geography: massive forests, giant rivers, waterfalls, hidden valleys, ancient trees, flower fields, mushroom forests, crystal caves, and living roots that form natural bridges.

Economy: exports medicinal herbs, rare flowers, living wood, healing potions, honey, fruit, natural dyes, and magical seeds. Imports steel, books, glass, and mining equipment.

Religion: nature itself — no gods, no temples. The World Tree is respected but not worshipped, and citizens believe every living thing has a spirit.

Festivals: Bloom Festival, Migration Celebration, Harvest Moon, Great Planting, Spirit Night, Festival of Leaves.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The World TreeLargest living organism on Solonia, age unknown — some believe it predates the Ancients
  • The Whispering MeadowFlowers sing at sunset, nobody knows why
  • Emerald FallsWater glows faintly at night
  • The Sleeping GroveNothing grows, nothing dies — animals refuse to enter
  • Crystal HollowAn underground forest lit entirely by glowing plants

Gameplay Identity: Eldergrove is about earning trust. Players who attack wildlife recklessly lose local reputation, while players who heal forests, rescue animals, and resolve conflicts peacefully unlock unique questlines, companions, and rewards.

The Great Mystery — The First Root: deep beneath the World Tree's roots lies a sealed chamber known only as the First Root. No Elder has entered the chamber, no map shows its location. Some scholars believe it's connected to the disappearance of the Ancients.

Pyrath

Also called The Eternal Forge, The Iron Kingdom, or The Mountain Kingdom — capital Emberforge, population ~7.4 million. A Forge Monarchy: the King rules, but the six Great Forge Houses each hold enormous influence.

National Identity: "A masterpiece is worth more than a thousand victories." Strength alone earns respect, but creating something legendary earns immortality.

Geography: built inside and around a vast volcanic mountain range — active volcanoes, lava rivers, obsidian valleys, steam caverns, ash plains, deep mines, magma lakes, underground cities, and massive stone bridges.

Economy: the largest industrial economy in Solonia. Exports weapons, armor, mining equipment, steel, rare ores, jewelry, mechanical devices, stone, and glass. Imports food, wood, herbs, livestock, books, and magic plants.

Religion: the Eternal Flame, believed to represent creation, not destruction — a forge is sacred because it transforms raw material into something greater.

Festivals: Festival of Sparks, Forge Championship, Builders' Week, Mining Day, Festival of Giants, Night of Embers.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Eternal FurnaceBurning continuously for over 2,000 years, never gone out
  • The Bridge of TitansThe largest bridge in Solonia, spanning an active lava canyon
  • The Obsidian CathedralCarved from volcanic glass — its walls reflect light like mirrors
  • The Hollow MountainEchoes voices from centuries ago, unexplained
  • The Deep ForgeA legendary forge hidden beneath Emberforge, rumored built by the Ancients

Gameplay Identity: the kingdom of creation and mastery. Players don't just come here to buy equipment — they come to build it, through commissions, upgrade stages, legendary blueprints, and forge house projects.

Aetheria

Also called The Kingdom Above the Clouds or The Crown of the Sky — capital Skyreach, population ~4.6 million. A Scholarly Monarchy: the Monarch rules alongside the Celestial Assembly, where the greatest scholars, mages, engineers, astronomers, and philosophers guide the kingdom's future.

National Identity: "Knowledge is the greatest form of power." Ignorance is considered far more dangerous than monsters.

Geography: most of Aetheria floats. Gigantic islands drift above the world, connected by cloud bridges, with wind currents serving as highways.

Economy: exports magic crystals, spell scrolls, airships, navigation instruments, enchanted tools, scientific instruments, weather devices, and sky silk. Imports food, wood, ore, stone, and medicinal plants.

Religion: the Celestial Path — a philosophy rather than a religion, believing the universe itself is the greatest teacher.

Festivals: Festival of Stars, Meteor Night, Sky Lantern Festival, Cloud Racing Championship, Three Moons Celebration, Inventor's Expo.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Grand ObservatoryLargest telescope in Solonia, can observe all three moons
  • The Celestial LibraryOver three million books — one sealed floor only the Queen may enter
  • The Crystal CascadeFloating waterfalls that flow upward before dissolving into clouds
  • The Hall of EchoesSpeak one sentence, the room answers with another
  • The Stairway to HeavenA floating staircase whose top nobody has reached

Gameplay Identity: the kingdom of discovery and advancement — knowledge itself becomes progression.

The Great Mystery — The Tenth Throne: beyond every floating island, astronomers occasionally record the silhouette of an impossible object — not a moon, not a star, not a cloud. Ancient journals call it only the Tenth Throne.

Khar'Duun

Also called The Golden Empire, The Endless Sands, or The First Throne — capital Sol'Kareth, population ~6.9 million. An Imperial Monarchy: the Emperor rules through twelve Viziers, each responsible for a different aspect of the empire.

National Identity: "The past is the greatest teacher." History is sacred, ruins are protected. Stealing artifacts is considered worse than stealing gold.

Geography: massive deserts, oases, rock canyons, ancient pyramids, sandstone cliffs, underground rivers, salt flats, and forgotten cities. The desert constantly changes.

Economy: exports spices, silk, glass, jewelry, dates, incense, rare gems, and licensed artifact replicas. Imports steel, timber, livestock, medicine, and books.

Religion: the Sun Father, the Moon Mother, and the River Spirits — balance between light, darkness, and water created civilization.

Festivals: Festival of Suns, Festival of Sands, Night of Lanterns, Day of Kings, Caravan Festival, The Great Excavation.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Golden PyramidLargest structure in Solonia — its lowest chamber has never been reached
  • The Valley of KingsBurial place of every emperor, most tombs still sealed
  • The Crystal OasisGlows beneath moonlight — its healing properties remain unexplained
  • The Whispering DunesVoices echo across the desert during strong winds
  • The Endless LabyrinthA buried underground city whose layout changes after sandstorms

Gameplay Identity: the kingdom of exploration and archaeology — many ruins change after sandstorms, so repeat visits reveal different layouts and secrets.

Blackfen

Also called The Untamed Kingdom, The Living Swamp, The Broken Crown, or The Endless Mire. No capital, no government — population unknown, estimated 2–5 million.

National Identity: "The swamp decides." There are no kings, no emperors, no nobles — every settlement governs itself.

Geography: swamps, mangroves, flooded forests, poison marshes, ancient ruins, bog lakes, dead forests, massive rivers, floating islands, and fog valleys. Maps become outdated constantly.

Economy: exports rare herbs, monster parts, poison, leather, fish, exotic plants, venom, and mushrooms. Imports steel, books, medicine, tools, and clothing.

Religion: none, officially. People believe in the Old Spirits — nobody worships them, they simply avoid angering them.

Festivals: Festival of Lanterns, Hunter's Moon, Great Catch, Fog Walk, Marsh Bloom, Night of Fireflies.

Famous Landmarks:

  • The Endless BogNobody has ever crossed it
  • The Hanging ForestTrees grow upside down
  • The Crooked RiverFlows in circles
  • The Giant's GraveThousands of enormous bones, creature unknown
  • The Singing MarshThe swamp sings during rain
  • The Hollow TreeLarge enough to contain an entire village

Gameplay Identity: unpredictability itself — no kingdom changes more often. Villages move, creatures migrate, NPCs travel, bridges collapse.

Creature Ecosystem (unique system): creatures interact with each other — too many crocodiles causes fish to drop, birds to migrate, snakes to grow aggressive, hunters to notice, and NPC quests to change in response.

The Great Mystery — The Heart Mire: a place no map has ever recorded, guarded by a stone door with nine empty circles: "When the Ninth Seal is broken, every kingdom will remember its first name."

Politics

Choose a kingdom to view its details:

Valoria

Government: A constitutional monarchy — the King rules, but must govern with the High Council: Lord Commander, Archmage, High Merchant, High Priest, Master Builder, High Judge, Royal Treasurer, Royal Spymaster.

Noble Houses — twelve exist; the most powerful are House Ashford, House Ravencrest, House Blackwood, House Evermere, House Thornfield, and House Holloway.

Military — the Royal Valorian Legion: Royal Knights, Longbow Corps, Mage Battalion, Heavy Cavalry, Scouts, Engineers, and the Royal Navy. Elite unit: the Silver Guard (200 members).

The Silent Crown (hidden) — the King's personal intelligence network.

The Crimson Jackals (criminal) — Valoria's largest crime syndicate: smuggling, assassinations, counterfeit artifacts, illegal monster trading.

Frosthaven

Government: King, Royal War Council, Council of Elders, Hunter Representatives, Mining Representatives, Church Representatives.

Military: largest standing army after Valoria's — Ice Legion, White Wolves, Glacier Rangers, Mountain Scouts, Royal Cavalry, Ice Mages. Elite unit: the Frostguard (300 members, never retreat).

Order of the White Flame (hidden) — protects ancient secrets beneath the glacier.

The Grey Hunters — independent monster hunters taking only impossible contracts.

The Ice Reavers (criminal) — smugglers, pirates, illegal crystal traders.

Diplomatic Relations:

  • ValoriaAllies — strong military alliance
  • Azure ReachFriendly — imports food, exports fish
  • EldergroveNeutral — rarely interact
  • PyrathRival — disagree over mining rights
  • MordrathDistrust — no king has entered willingly

Kingdom Reputation: helping Frosthaven gains forge discounts, better hunting contracts, elite trainers, and festival invites; betraying it can close off entire quest lines.

Mordrath

Government: instead of ministers, Mordrath has Keepers — of Knowledge, Shadows, Silence, Dreams, and Names (names sometimes disappear from records).

The Circle of Ash (hidden) — ancient historians who believe history is being rewritten.

Children of Twilight — operate orphanages, secretly monitor children with unusual abilities.

The Veiled Hand — assassins who kill only to preserve "the balance."

The Silent Market (criminal) — a black market appearing only on certain unpredictable nights.

Diplomatic Relations: distrusted by almost every kingdom. Azure Reach quietly trades despite denials; Aetheria studies it under heavy supervision; Frosthaven patrols but avoids entering.

Azure Reach

Government — the Maritime Council (elected every ten years): Grand Admiral, Master Navigator, Trade Minister, Fleet Commander, Explorer Representative, Merchant Representative, Harbor Master, Sea Scholar, Weather Sage.
