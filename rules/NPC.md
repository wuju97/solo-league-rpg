# NPC RULES

## Population Structure
Every NPC belongs somewhere: World → Kingdom → City → District → Home → Occupation →
Faction → Friends → Family → Enemies → Quest Pool → Daily Schedule.

## NPC Tiers
- **Tier 1 — Legendary NPCs** (~100): kings, guild masters, legendary adventurers,
  ancient beings. Can change the world.
- **Tier 2 — Major NPCs** (~500): master blacksmith, famous merchant, arena champion,
  guild captain, and similar.
- **Tier 3 — Citizens** (1000+): farmers, children, teachers, guards, musicians, bakers,
  doctors, artists.

## Occupation System
Over 120 professions exist: blacksmith, weaponsmith, armorer, tailor, doctor, healer,
alchemist, librarian, teacher, miner, engineer, architect, farmer, fisherman, hunter,
explorer, navigator, priest, mage, scholar, historian, caravan guard, treasure hunter,
monster researcher, chef, innkeeper, musician, painter, glassmaker, potter, jeweler,
shipwright, cartographer, messenger, judge, executioner, diplomat, spy, assassin,
florist, beekeeper, horse breeder, dragon keeper, falcon trainer, and many more.

## NPC Memory System
Important NPCs remember player actions. Save a merchant, and months later he
acknowledges it instead of greeting you generically. Ignore, steal from, or harm an
NPC (or their family) and they — and their family — remember.

## Family Trees & Friendships
Important NPCs have real families (spouse, children, siblings, parents) and social
webs (friends, rivals, connections to other NPCs). Killing an NPC ripples outward —
family loses them, business closes, town economy shifts, friends want revenge.

## Schedules
NPCs run real daily routines (wake, eat, work, tavern, sleep) that shift on weekends,
during festivals, in rain, and during world events — not static idle poses.

## Dialogue Layers & Quest Pools
Seven relationship layers: First Meeting → Friendly → Trusted → Best Friend →
Legendary Hero → Enemy → Hated. Each important NPC has 10-30 quests in rotation
instead of one repeating quest, spaced out over months.

## Children, Merchants, Adventurers
Children are real NPCs who grow socially over time based on world events (can become
knights, merchants, scholars, villains, explorers). Merchants travel real routes
between kingdoms on a schedule players can learn. The world has its own independent
adventurers (S-Rank Explorers, Dragon Hunters) who become famous, disappear, or die
without player involvement.

## Relationship Tracking
Every NPC tracks: Friendship, Respect, Trust, Fear, Suspicion, Debt, Favor, Fame,
Reputation. Affects dialogue, prices, quests, alliances.

## Death & Marriage
Important NPCs rarely die randomly but can (e.g. a village falling). NPCs can marry,
have children, retire, move cities, start businesses, become leaders, go bankrupt, or
turn to crime or heroism — all independent of the player.

## Festivals & Rumors
NPCs celebrate during festivals instead of working. Rumors spread gradually between
kingdoms (not instantly) to keep the world feeling geographically real. Every kingdom
has a Chronicle Keepers organization recording history as it happens — meaning the
in-world history is written by what actually occurs, not just pre-authored content.
