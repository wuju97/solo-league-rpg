Gameplay

Game Rules

Starting Out

You pick a class at character creation (Knight, Berserker, Assassin, Ranger, Arcanist, Druid, Paladin, Necromancer, Monk, Bard, Alchemist, or Beastmaster). Your class sets your base Power, Defense, Speed, and HP, plus one rank-scaling passive. Every player starts locked to Valoria at E-rank, with only Valoria unlocked.

Class Growth by Rank

Your class's base stats and your Player Card's 3 abilities both scale up as your rank rises (E through SS) — not by level, by rank specifically. Each class grows differently: some (Paladin, Druid, Knight) lean tanky with high HP and Defense but low Speed; others (Assassin, Monk) lean pure speed, exceeding even a Kingdom Champion's typical Speed by SS-rank; others (Arcanist, Necromancer) stay glass-cannons even at the top, with Power beyond Champion-level but HP and Defense that stay comparatively low. By SS-rank, every class is roughly comparable in overall power to a Kingdom Champion, just arrived at differently. Choose carefully — your class becomes permanent the moment you enter your first political hierarchy fight (Warriors through Champion). Creature fights and Primordial fights don't lock it.

Class Passives

Every class has one passive that scales with rank — weaker at E, strongest at SS — and works identically no matter which fight type you're in (solo, PvP, party, or boss). All twelve:

  • Knight — Last StandReduced damage below 40% HP
  • Berserker — Blood RageBonus damage from missing HP, costs HP per attack
  • Assassin — ExecutionGuaranteed crit vs. enemies below 25% HP, every 4 turns
  • Ranger — DeadeyeEvery 4th attack ignores a portion of Defense
  • Arcanist — Arcane OverflowBonus spell damage above 50% HP; every 5th spell free
  • Druid — Nature's CycleHealing restores resource, damage restores HP
  • Paladin — Sacred ResolveShield from any healing received
  • Necromancer — Soul HarvestBuilds Souls from damage; free empowered ability at 100
  • Monk — Flow StateAttack chains build Flow (up to 3), boosting the next hit — Guard or using an ability resets it
  • Bard — EncoreEvery 5th ability auto-repeats at reduced effectiveness
  • Alchemist — Corrosive MixturesDamaging abilities stack Defense reduction on the target
  • Beastmaster — Pack BondCompanion deals bonus damage above 50% HP

Beastmaster also lets you name your companion, either at character creation or when switching into the class.

Cards

A card is a single ability, not a whole creature. Your Player Card — your class's 3 abilities — is automatically included in every fight, on top of up to 3 optional creature cards you choose beforehand (0-3, not mandatory — your Player Card alone is enough to fight). Creature cards are earned through the Ability-Fragment system: defeating a creature grants one of its abilities at a time (weakest first), with the unlock chance dropping as abilities get stronger (70% / 45% / 25% / 10%), and a pity bonus that grows on repeated failures. Cards render as actual card-shaped visuals everywhere they appear — Pre-Battle selection, live battle, and your Player tab.

Combat

Turn order is Speed-based — whoever's faster (base Speed plus any temporary bonus) acts first, every exchange, in every fight type, including the enemy potentially acting before you if it's faster. Each turn you choose to Attack, Guard, or use one of your cards' abilities, gated by that card's own energy (shown as a real fillable bar on each card, not just a number). Damage follows Power minus Defense. The Elemental Wheel (Fire → Ice → Nature → Sand → Water → Storm → Poison → Shadow → Light → back to Fire) grants +25% damage against the next element in the cycle — when it triggers, the battle log calls it out directly ("The elements favor you!"). Elements also carry signature status effects — Burn, Freeze, Root, Blind, Soak, Shock, Poison, Fear, and Purify.

Beyond Damage — Full Ability Effects

Abilities aren't just damage numbers. Temporary stat buffs and debuffs, shields that absorb incoming hits, dodge chance, damage reflection, one-time revives, defense-piercing, conditional bonus damage, HP/energy steals, forced skip-turns, and even copying an opponent's last-used ability are all real, working effects — not flavor text. These work the same way whether it's you, an enemy, a party member, or an opponent in PvP using them.

Encounters

Roam gives random creature encounters weighted by rarity (E-C common, B-A less frequent, S-SS rare). Targeted Hunts let you track a specific creature directly once you've partially learned its abilities. Political hierarchies (Warriors → Captains → Nobles → Generals → King → Champion) are climbed by defeating every named NPC in a tier before the next unlocks, with a cooldown before you can re-fight someone (2 days, 3 for Champions). You must physically be in that kingdom to fight its hierarchy — checked before the fight starts, not just at the end. Defeating a Champion resets that kingdom's ladder, requiring a full re-climb to challenge them again.

PKMN, NRTO, LOL — Traveler Mini-Games

Travel the Roads, and a traveler might offer you a riddle instead of a fight. Three guessing games, each built the same way: a name is picked in secret, you type a guess, and the game tells you which attributes of your guess match the hidden answer and which don't — keep guessing until you land on it exactly. These are outside Solonia's own lore entirely — real Pokemon, real Naruto characters, real League of Legends champions.

PKMN — guess a real Pokemon. Compares primary Type, secondary Type, Generation, and Legendary status.
NRTO — guess a real Naruto character. Compares Village, Rank, and Nature (elemental) Type.
LOL — guess a real League of Legends champion. Compares Role, Region, Gender, and Release Era.

Start typing in the guess box and only real, valid names will show up as suggestions — you're always picking from an actual list, never guessing blind at spelling. Solve it and you're paid in gold, same as any other traveler favor.

Losing Has Real Stakes

Losing to a political hierarchy NPC (Warriors through Champion) costs real XP — more for losing to someone higher up the ladder. If that XP loss drops you below your current level's threshold, your level falls too, and a big enough drop can cost you a rank. Surrendering any fight costs gold instead. Creature fights and Primordial fights carry no such penalty on a loss. Whatever you lost — XP, gold, a level, a rank — is shown directly on the defeat screen.

Rank & Unlocking

Rank comes from XP, earned in any fight — creature, hierarchy, PvP, or League KDA sync — and the XP needed per level genuinely climbs the higher you go. Leveling up raises your rank (E through SS), and reaching certain ranks unlocks new countries at random from whichever you haven't reached yet (D→1, C→2, B→2, A→3, all 9 by A-rank). Higher-rank creatures are gated behind total countries unlocked: B/A-rank need 3+ countries, S-rank needs 5+, SS-rank needs 7+.

Gear

Weapons and armor add real stat bonuses in a fight (weapon boosts Attack, armor reduces incoming damage passively), pulled from your actual Shop catalog. Consumables can be used at the start of a fight and genuinely heal. All three are chosen per-fight from the Pre-Battle screen — your permanent Inventory equip state is separate and unaffected, so you can bring different gear to different fights without changing what's equipped.

Parties & PvP

Party fights use simultaneous submission — every living member picks their action, and once everyone's in, the whole round resolves together in true Speed order alongside the enemy, whose turn can land in the middle of your party's turns if it's fast enough. PvP duels (via Duel Trade) use the exact same engine — cards, classes, energy, full ability effects — with the faster player acting first each exchange. The Elemental Wheel bonus never applies in PvP specifically, since players carry no element of their own.

Attribute Points

Attribute Points (AP) come from two sources. Leveling up grants a flat 5 AP per level, on top of whatever XP source triggered it. Every single fight you win also grants AP on its own, based on your current rank at the moment of victory — E, D, and C-rank wins grant 3 AP, B and A-rank grant 5, S-rank grants 7, and SS-rank grants 9. Party wins grant this to every participating member, not just whoever landed the last hit. Spend AP on your stats or convert it to gold from the Stats & Attributes tab.

Duel Chat Tabs

Duel Chat splits fights into three separate tabs by opponent type. PvE covers ordinary creatures and regular political hierarchy NPCs (Warriors through King), with its own Creature and Characters sub-tabs. PvM is exclusively for Kingdom Champions — Monarchs. PvG is exclusively for Primordials. A Monarch fight never appears in PvE, and a Primordial fight never appears anywhere but PvG.

Before a duel starts, either player can lock in a bet — gold, AP, and/or specific items — which gets held immediately once locked. When the duel ends, the winner takes the entire combined pot from both sides. Changed your mind before the fight starts? Cancel Duel in the Prep screen refunds anything already locked in.

Separately, Duel Trade also supports genuine gifting — offer gold, AP, any number of items, and any number of ability cards to another player by name, no fight required. They can accept or reject; nothing changes hands until they do.

Game Objective

The long arc of what you're actually working toward.

Short Term

Fight, earn XP and ability-fragment cards, and grow your class's power. Every fight — creature, hierarchy, or PvP — feeds the same rank progression.

Mid Term

Climb each kingdom's political hierarchy from Warriors up to its Champion, and unlock new kingdoms as your rank rises. Each kingdom has two separate tracks — its creatures (fought by anyone, anytime) and its political ladder (gated, climbed in order).

How Countries Unlock

You start locked to Valoria only. From there, new kingdoms unlock automatically as your rank rises from XP — not chosen by you, picked at random from whichever kingdoms you haven't reached yet:

  • Reach D-rankunlocks 1 kingdom (2 total)
  • Reach C-rankunlocks 2 more (4 total)
  • Reach B-rankunlocks 2 more (6 total)
  • Reach A-rankunlocks the final 3 (all 9)

Creature Rank Gating

Even once a creature is in front of you, its rank can still be locked based on how many countries you've unlocked in total, not which kingdom you're standing in:

  • B and A-rank creaturesrequire 3+ countries unlocked
  • S-rank creaturesrequire 5+ countries unlocked
  • SS-rank creaturesrequire 7+ countries unlocked

E, D, and C-rank creatures are never gated this way — fightable from the very start.

Conquest

Defeating a kingdom's Champion grants real ownership of that kingdom — the country's true top authority, above even its King. Ownership can be lost if someone else defeats that Champion after you.

Champion's Mystery Reward

Beating a Champion also gives you a choice: 3 face-down cards, and you pick one. Two hold gold (1500 or 2500), one holds a genuine random ability belonging to the Champion you just beat — added straight to your card collection if you get it. Every pick is freshly random, every time, for every Champion — there's no pattern to learn or exploit.

Endgame — PvG

The Primordial Gods, one per kingdom, are the true endgame. Reaching them requires three things at once: every one of the 9 kingdoms conquered, every one of the 9 Champions defeated, and every SS-rank creature across every kingdom defeated at least once. Once eligible, click any kingdom's name on the World Map to challenge its Primordial directly.

Every Primordial fight requires a party at first — solo access to the remaining Primordials only opens up after you've personally been part of a party that's defeated 1 Primordial. Defeating a Primordial grants triple the usual SS-rank XP guaranteed, plus a choice: 4 face-down cards, pick one — a genuine random ability belonging to that Primordial, 20,000 Gold, 200 Attribute Points, or 500 bonus XP. The card positions are freshly shuffled every single time, so there's no pattern to learn.
